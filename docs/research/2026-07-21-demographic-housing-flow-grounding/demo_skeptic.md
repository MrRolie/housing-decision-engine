== THE EPISTEMIC SKEPTIC'S ATTACK ==
Target claim: "This model can estimate the likelihood of a plex-market crash in Québec/Montréal over the coming decades."
Verdict up front: the claim as literally stated is unsupportable and reproduces the exact error that has killed this model class three times in 35 years. A reduced form is worth building. The full form is Mankiw-Weil 2.0 with a Québec data handicap.

────────────────────────────────────────
1. FAILURE AUTOPSY, APPLIED TO QUÉBEC PLEXES
────────────────────────────────────────
Every mechanism that broke the Mankiw-Weil class applies to Québec/Montréal plexes with FULL force, and one of them is worse here than anywhere else in Canada. Rated:

• Cohort-vs-age confound — FULL force, and DATA-BLOCKED here specifically. This was the single biggest M-W killer: the "demand declines after 40" pattern was poorer earlier cohorts, not a true aging effect. The one methodological fix that made Myers/Fannie Mae defensible (track cohort HOMEOWNERSHIP RETENTION rates over time, not cross-sectional age-price) is precisely what Québec data cannot support: CHSP excludes Québec structurally, so no owner-level cohort tracking exists for the province. A Québec build is forced back onto cross-sectional Census tenure-by-age — the exact input that bakes in the confound M-W died on. Québec is the worst-instrumented province in Canada for the ONE method that separates defensible from indefensible. This alone is close to disqualifying for an owner-cohort model.

• Income/wealth growth — FULL force. Québec boomer owners are wealthier and healthier; the assumed age-tenure profile is non-stationary in exactly the direction that reverses the crash signal.

• Interest-rate / financing / rate lock-in — FULL force and DOMINATES the horizon. Rates change faster than demographics and flipped the SIGN of the near-term effect even in Myers' careful model (he wrote pre-2022 and got run late by rate lock-in he couldn't model). Canada makes this WORSE, not better, for a demographic model: 5-year renewal mortgages mean Canadian lock-in is weaker than the US 30-year, so the financing channel repices every 5 years and dominates demographics even more heavily. A 15-25yr plex hold spans 3-5 full rate cycles; demographic drift is a rounding error against rate-cycle amplitude over that window. A demographic crash model that omits a financing-conditions term is omitting the actual crash generator.

• Supply elasticity — FULL force, direction-specific. CMHC names construction/approval constraints as the dominant bottleneck (2.6M-unit gap). Inelastic supply means demand shifts hit PRICE not quantity — amplifying, cutting both ways. Plex-specific wrinkle: the plex stock is largely pre-1960 and effectively fixed-to-shrinking (de-conversion / condo-ization removes plexes faster than any get built under current zoning). So plex supply is near-perfectly inelastic AND structurally declining REGARDLESS of demographics — a confound the model would misattribute to demographic demand.

• Aging-in-place / preference shift — FULL force, empirically running AGAINST the crash thesis. The crash story needs boomers to sell. CMHC Québec data shows the opposite: age-75+ senior selling rate FELL 38.6%→36% (1996-2001 vs 2016-2021), and Montréal seniors skew to RENTAL not condo-purchase. The US "silver tsunami" has not materialized as inventory (78% plan to stay). The behavioral sell-rate the model most depends on is exactly the parameter that is non-stationary, mismeasured, and moving the wrong way.

• Migration inflows — FULL force AND it is the load-bearing input (see §2). It is the demand offset the entire "does immigrant buy-demand compensate boomer sell-wave" question hinges on, and it is the least forecastable input in the system.

Net: the model class fails for six identifiable reasons; all six apply here at full strength; the cohort-retention fix is uniquely unavailable in Québec; and the dominant crash driver (rates/credit) is out of the model's scope entirely. The demographic wave, moreover, is the WRONG SHAPE for "crash" — Japan shows demographics produce slow periphery grind (9M vacant rural homes, akiya), not tail events; crashes are fast rate/credit/recession repricings. Using the slowest-moving variable in the system to estimate the fastest-moving outcome is a timescale category error.

────────────────────────────────────────
2. THE POLICY-ENDOGENEITY PROBLEM
────────────────────────────────────────
Immigration is the demand-side input the model most needs and least can forecast, and it is not a demographic quantity — it is a POLICY VARIABLE chosen by two governments on a ~annual cadence.

• It is doubly policy-controlled in Québec: federal caps PLUS Québec's own MIFI levels plan (45,000 PR/yr set Nov 2025, and for the FIRST TIME temporary-resident caps — a lever that did not exist before 2026).
• It swings by large factors by fiat: federal temporary-resident intake was cut 673,650→385,000 in a single year (−43%). That is not a variable you extrapolate; it is a variable that gets legislated.
• A 20-year immigration path is a forecast of ~5+ election cycles across two levels of government, plus Québec sovereignty/identity politics that make its immigration lever MORE exogenous and more volatile than the rest of Canada's.

What this reduces the model's claims to: an unconditional "P(crash) = z%" over decades is a disguised political forecast — the probability is doing no epistemic work, it is smuggling in an unjustified guess about future policy and laundering it as demography. The honest object the model can produce is a CONDITIONAL response surface — "IF immigration runs at rate X, THEN net flow imbalance is Y" — a map indexed by policy. Collapsing that map to one probability requires a weighting over policy paths the model has zero basis to supply.

Two further poisons on the unconditional claim:
• Correlated conditioners: immigration, rates, and recession are not independent (Desjardins: immigration drawdown + tariff recession COMPOUND). You cannot even marginalize the policy axis independently.
• Reflexivity: immigration is a STABILIZER that responds to the predicted outcome. CMHC explicitly frames immigration as the affordability swing lever — if a crash looms, government can turn immigration UP to prevent it. The forcing variable is a feedback controller reacting to the very outcome being forecast. Unconditional crash-probability from a feedback-controlled input is not merely uncertain; it is ill-posed.

────────────────────────────────────────
3. WHAT THE MODEL CAN HONESTLY SUPPORT — THE LINE, DRAWN PRECISELY
────────────────────────────────────────
CAN (conditional, relative, monitored):
• Directional flow-imbalance SCENARIOS — given assumed immigration + assumed senior-exit rate, compute the SIGN and rough magnitude of net owner-household flow by geography. Conditional, not probabilistic.
• Tail-scenario construction for stress testing — build the internally-consistent "bad corner" (immigration cut to MIFI floor + accelerated 85+ exits in the 2030-2040 surge window + heir forced-sale delay-then-list + rate shock) to produce a PRINCIPLED shock-parameter set for the housing-decision-engine's S4b Monte-Carlo. This is the model's actual product: S4b today has "no principled way to set shock parameters" — a coherent demographic scenario grid fills exactly that hole. Real, bounded value.
• Relative geography/segment RANKINGS — where demographic risk CONCENTRATES. This is the strongest honest output because the common-mode confounds (rates, income) partially difference out across segments. Load-bearing findings to encode: periphery/off-island (natural decrease now REALIZED — 80,450 deaths vs 78,200 births 2025; interprovincial outflow ~7,600) carries structural demand erosion; and the Montréal-specific INVERSION — one projection has Montréal SHRINKING ~4.5% while Québec City GROWS ~21%, i.e. the traditional "core" is the loser here, opposite the Tokyo/NYC intuition. Do not assume "Montréal = safe core metro" by analogy. Plex vs condo: plexes are a shrinking, gentrification-bid asset in transit-adjacent boroughs; the senior-downsizing target is rental in Montréal, not condo purchase.
• Leading-indicator TRIPWIRES — convert the un-forecastable into a MONITORED signal (a monitored-signal pattern, correctly applied). Watch the realized values you cannot predict: IRCC PR-by-CMA landings, temporary-resident stock vs MIFI plan, Registre foncier transfer volumes by administrative region, CMHC senior-sale-rate refreshes, natural-increase sign. Don't predict the political number — watch it and react.

CANNOT:
• An unconditional P(crash) over decades (§2).
• A point price forecast — the entire literature's graveyard.
• Any Québec owner-level cohort-retention estimate — CHSP-blocked; even the conditional senior-exit rate must be IMPORTED from national CMHC proxies, so the model's own behavioral core is borrowed, not measured.

THE LINE: conditional, relative, and monitored — YES. Unconditional, absolute, and predictive — NO. It is a scenario-and-tripwire engine, never a probability engine.

────────────────────────────────────────
4. DECISION-RELEVANCE TEST (personal plex, 15-25yr hold)
────────────────────────────────────────
Generic prudence already gives: "Can I carry the mortgage through a 30% price drop + rate shock + a vacancy spell, and hold 20 years? If yes, buy; the drawdown is survivable and I hold through it." For a single transaction with the exit two decades out, and a literature that says demographic effects wash out or reverse over that horizon while rates dominate the cycles you actually live through, the buy/don't-buy binary is essentially settled by the drawdown-survival test alone.

Does any plausible model output move that decision? Tested honestly:
• Buy/don't-buy binary: ~ZERO marginal value over "can I survive a 30% drawdown and hold." Demographics are slow-grind, not crash-shaped; the tail risk that the 30% test is calibrated to is rate/credit/recession, which this model does not touch.
• WHICH property / WHICH borough: MODEST value — the one place marginal value plausibly lives. A credible ranking (periphery erosion vs core-transit gentrification bid; Montréal-shrinks-QC-grows inversion; plex as shrinking asset class) changes the property selection, an actionable margin. BUT it is largely duplicative of cheap local heuristics ("buy near transit/jobs, avoid shrinking regions") a realtor gives free.
• Stress MAGNITUDE: asymmetric and dangerous to lean on. The model would tend to REDUCE the implied stress for core Montréal (shrinking supply + gentrification bid) — exactly the direction where you must NOT let a model talk you into less caution. Using it to ADD a specific geographic caution (off-island, single-region-dependent plex) is fine; using it to RELAX the 30% is the failure mode.
• Timing: NONE. Unforecastable (immigration + rates), and a 20-year hold makes timing moot anyway.

Concrete marginal decision value: near-zero on the binary, modest-but-mostly-redundant on property selection, negative if used to relax prudence. The model answers a DIFFERENT question (slow demographic drift and its geographic dispersion) than the one asked (crash likelihood). That is the core indictment: it is a well-posed tool pointed at the wrong question.

────────────────────────────────────────
5. VERDICT: BUILD-REDUCED
────────────────────────────────────────
Don't-build: the "crash-likelihood-over-decades estimator" as literally requested. Four decisive reasons — (1) load-bearing input (immigration) is an unforecastable 20-yr political variable that also reflexively responds to the predicted outcome; (2) the cohort-retention fix separating defensible from indefensible is data-blocked in Québec (CHSP-excluded); (3) demographics are the wrong timescale for "crash" — slow grind, not tail event; (4) for the actual 15-25yr buy decision it adds ~zero over the drawdown-survival test already in use.

Build-reduced — REDUCED FORM SPEC:
Build a conditional scenario + tripwire module feeding the housing-decision-engine S4b layer, scoped to exactly three outputs and forbidden a fourth:
  (a) POLICY-INDEXED flow-imbalance scenario generator → produces internally-consistent shock-parameter sets for S4b's Monte-Carlo (fills the real, named gap: S4b has "no principled way to set shocks").
  (b) RELATIVE geography/segment risk-ranking (periphery vs core, off-island vs on-island, plex vs condo) with the Montréal-shrinks / QC-City-grows inversion flagged as a headline caution.
  (c) A small TRIPWIRE set monitoring realized values of the un-forecastable inputs (PR-by-CMA, temp-resident stock vs MIFI, Registre foncier transfers, natural-increase sign, CMHC senior-sale-rate).
  (FORBIDDEN) any unconditional P(crash) or point price forecast. Impose the same discipline a prior internal review imposed on its own demographic layer: "informs, no capture fraction" → here, "informs the buy-geography and the stress magnitude; does not predict the crash."

Altitude / reuse discipline (guard against over-engineering a personal buy decision):
• Reuse actuarial-system's CPM2014/CPM-B Québec mortality tables as the death-decrement leg only.
• Reuse Lee-Carter/CBD as a TEMPLATE (not import) for any hazard surface; reuse the earlier internal cohort-loader PATTERN by copying it, never by import.
• Do NOT build a multi-state/multiple-decrement competing-hazards engine from scratch — none exists in actuarial-system, and a simple deterministic cohort roll-forward with sensitivity bands is the correct altitude for a personal decision + showcase. Building the full multi-decrement machinery would be exactly the scope-creep-toward-a-graveyard the §1 autopsy warns against.

LinkedIn showcase note: this IS presentable as genuinely original work — no rigorous Québec study nets boomer-release against immigrant-absorption (confirmed literature gap). But the honest showcase is "a disciplined, policy-indexed scenario + tripwire map of an un-studied question — and here is why anyone quoting you a crash PROBABILITY is selling Mankiw-Weil 2.0." A showcase that claims to predict crash likelihood joins the public graveyard; a showcase that shows the scenario map AND names the epistemic limit is both defensible and more impressive.
