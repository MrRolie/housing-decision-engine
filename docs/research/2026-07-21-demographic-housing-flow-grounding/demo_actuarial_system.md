1. Mortality/life-table machinery — what actually exists and works: `mcp_server/engine/mortality.py` is a real, tested, pluggable base-table + improvement-scale engine. It registers four base tables backed by real CSVs in `mcp_server/data/mortality_tables/`: US SOA RP-2014 combined (male/female), and three CIA CPM2014 variants (combined, public, private) for Canada/Québec. Improvement scales: US MP-2021 and Canadian CPM-B (sex-specific). `get_qx(age, gender, calendar_year)` interpolates gaps in the raw table and applies cumulative annual improvement factors from the table's base year forward — this is the standard "base table x improvement scale" projection approach, correctly implemented, and covered by `tests/test_mortality.py` (part of the 76 passing tests). It is strictly an *individual* API: one age/gender/year in, one q_x out. There is no cohort/population batch interface — calling it for a population means looping externally.

Basis selection is a global, not a parameter: `_active_base`/`_active_scale` module-level state, mutated by `set_active_mortality()`, defaulting to the US pair unless a plan/caller explicitly sets the Canadian basis. The file's own docstring flags this as a known single-threaded-only limitation (a v2 ContextVar refactor is roadmapped but not built) — directly relevant if a housing model wanted to vectorize/parallelize across many households or cohorts.
