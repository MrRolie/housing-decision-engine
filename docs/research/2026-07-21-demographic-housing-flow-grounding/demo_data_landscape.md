## Inventory: machine-readable data for a Québec (Montréal) demographic housing-flow model

### 1. Statistics Canada CHSP (Canadian Housing Statistics Program) — QUÉBEC IS EXCLUDED

**Coverage:** CHSP (StatCan survey SDDS 5257, table family 46-10-00xx / 46-26-00xx, analytical series 46-28-0001) covers residential properties and owners in **all provinces/territories except Québec**. This has been true since the program's inception (rollout order was roughly BC/ON first, then NS/NB/PEI/MB/AB/NL/territories added over successive cycles) and remains true through the most recent 2025 republication (Sept 25 2025, 2022 reference year — that specific release also temporarily excluded Saskatchewan). The 2024-reference-year release is scheduled for Mar 31 2026 and there is no public documentation of a Québec-inclusion date. Treat this as a **structural, not transitional, gap** — CHSP is built by Statistics Canada linking *provincial* property assessment rolls / land-title registries to Census, tax, Business Register, and Longitudinal Immigration Database microdata; Québec has not entered the data-sharing arrangement that makes this linkage legally possible, and no source found explains why or projects when. Do not plan around an assumed near-term fix.

**What CHSP publishes where it does operate (i.e., NOT Québec):** owner-level age, sex, family type, immigration status (including years-since-landing and non-permanent-resident status), income (via tax linkage), number of properties owned (single vs. multiple), assessment value, and property/dwelling type occupied. Example: table "Single and multiple residential property owners by immigration characteristics" (46-10-0052-01, now marked inactive/superseded but archived). Granularity: linked microdata resolves to **census subdivision (CSD)** — 2,955 CSDs across the 12 covered jurisdictions — with most published aggregate tables released at province and CMA level. Annual cadence. Access: free public tables via StatCan Data Table Viewer (no paywall); underlying linked microdata is RDC (Research Data Centre)-access only, not open. A 2025 CHSP-linked study ("Non-permanent residents in the homeownership market," 46-28-0001-2025001) and a June 2026 study ("The homeownership trajectories of recent immigrants," 46-28-0001-2026001) both explicitly restrict their province lists to exclude Québec (and sometimes NL/Saskatchewan) "because homeownership data were not available."

**Practical implication for Montréal modeling:** the single richest StatCan product for "who owns, by age/immigration status, and are they multi-property owners" simply does not exist for Québec/Montréal. The Québec side of an owner-demographic model cannot lean on CHSP at all and must be built from (a) Census cross-tabs (item 2) at the household level, not the property-owner level, plus (b) Québec's own open property-assessment data (item 6) which has parcel/CUBF/value/construction-year but NO owner-demographic fields (age, immigration status) attached — that owner-level enrichment is exactly the missing CHSP step.

### 2. Census of Population — homeownership × age × immigration, by geography

- **Standard published tables (free, no request needed):** StatCan's 2021 Census profile/"focus on geography" pages publish **homeownership rate by age of primary household maintainer** at CMA, census division (CD), and city/municipality level (e.g., Montréal CMA, individual boroughs where they equal CSDs) for census years 2011–2021, in ~5 age bands (<35, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65-74, 75+ roughly). This is free, downloadable via www12.statcan.gc.ca census profile tool or bulk CSV via the Census Profile / "Aggregate Dissemination Areas" catalogue product (98-401-X2021012).
- **Immigrant tenure cross-tabs:** Published analytical series (Housing Statistics in Canada, cat. 46-28-0001) includes "Households with multiple maintainers and impact on homeownership" (2024001) and immigrant-specific studies, generally at **province/CMA** granularity, occasionally CD. True "ownership rate by years-since-landing × admission category (economic/family/refugee) × CMA" cross-tabs exist in the recent (June 2026) StatCan study on immigrant homeownership trajectories — but that study is CHSP-linked-microdata-based and **explicitly excludes Québec** for the same reason as item 1.
- **Census-tract / dissemination-area (DA) level:** Standard published Census Profile products go down to **census tract and DA** for basic tenure (owner/renter counts) but the age-of-maintainer × tenure cross-tab is **not published at CT/DA level** in the free standard tables — only at CD/CMA and above. Getting age-of-maintainer-by-tenure at CT or DA level for Montréal requires a **custom tabulation request** to Statistics Canada (paid service — cost quoted case-by-case, not publicly listed; StatCan's custom-tab service tabulates from the 100%-short-form database for any content/geography/format combination a user specifies). This is the correct fallback path since CHSP is unavailable for Québec.
- Immigration/citizenship/admission-category variables ARE part of the 2021 Census long-form/100% content, so a custom tab combining tenure × age-of-maintainer × immigrant-admission-category × landing-cohort at CT level for Montréal CMA is technically constructible — just not free and not pre-published.

### 3. ISQ (Institut de la statistique du Québec) — population AND household projections

- **Population projections:** "Perspectives démographiques du Québec et de ses régions" — current edition (2024) covers **2021–2071** (superseding the earlier 2019 edition's 2016–2066 horizon and a 2021 update). Reference scenario plus low/high variants ("scénario de référence," plus explicit high-growth / low-growth / other analytical scenarios). Geographic levels: Québec total, the 17 administrative regions (régions administratives), RMR (metropolitan areas, i.e., CMA equivalents — Montréal RMR is covered), and MRC (regional county municipalities, the finest published level; a 2025 methodological update refreshed MRC-level projections). Age-group detail by single/5-year bands through the full 0–100+ range for population projections.
- **Household projections ("Projections de ménages"):** Separate ISQ product, derived by applying headship/reference-person rates by age group to the population projections. Horizon **2021–2071** (2026-edition PDF confirmed), with the same reference/high/low scenario structure. Geographic levels: Québec total, administrative regions, **RMR (metropolitan areas — Montréal RMR available)**, and **MRC**. Age breakdown is by **age of household reference person (soutien économique / personne de référence)**, not full age-of-maintainer-by-tenure detail — i.e., it gives you *how many households* by age-of-reference-person by geography, not homeownership status directly; tenure must be layered on from Census cross-tabs (item 2).
- **Access/cost:** All free, downloadable PDF/Excel from statistique.quebec.ca. No paywall, no API — bulk files only, updated on an irregular multi-year cadence (methodological updates roughly every 2-3 years; most recent MRC-level refresh 2025, headline population projection edition 2024/2026).
- **Granularity gap vs. Montréal boroughs (arrondissements):** ISQ's finest published geography is MRC — the Montréal agglomeration's boroughs are NOT separately projected by ISQ (Montréal city itself constitutes some MRC-equivalent geographies, but sub-city borough-level household/age projections do not exist as an ISQ product). Borough-level granularity would have to come from the municipal property-assessment roll (item 6) combined with Census tract data (item 2), not from ISQ projections.

### 4. Immigration flows — IRCC + Québec MIFI

- **IRCC federal open data (open.canada.ca, free, bulk CSV/XLS, updated monthly):**
  - "Permanent Residents – Monthly IRCC Updates" — admissions by Province/Territory × Immigration Category (economic/family/refugee/other), and separately by Province/Territory × CMA. Montréal CMA is a distinct row.
  - "Permanent Residents – Ad Hoc IRCC (Specialized Datasets)" — finer historical breakdowns, e.g. admissions by Country of Citizenship × CMA of intended destination, back to ~2013 (used e.g. for Vancouver/BC in the sample resource found; Montréal/Québec equivalents exist in the same dataset family).
  - Values are rounded to nearest 5 and suppressed below 5 for confidentiality (standard StatCan/IRCC disclosure control) — fine granularity (e.g., single-CMA × single-category × single-year small cells) will show as suppressed.
  - Express Entry-specific admissions dataset also exists separately (federal economic stream; largely inapplicable to Québec since Québec runs its own economic selection).
- **Québec MIFI (provincial):** Québec selects its own economic-class immigrants (Quebec Skilled Worker / Programme de l'expérience québécoise) via **Certificat de sélection du Québec (CSQ)**. Open data: "Number of Quebec selection certificates (CSQ) issued by immigration program, 2016–2023" (donneesquebec.ca / open.canada.ca), free bulk CSV. This is the Québec-specific selection-stage data; actual PR *landing* counts by CMA still come from the federal IRCC PR-by-CMA dataset (item above), since IRCC executes final admission regardless of who selected the applicant.
- **MIFI Plan d'immigration 2026-2029 (released Nov 6 2025):** targets ~45,000 permanent admissions/year for Québec (a reduction from prior plans). For the **first time**, Québec's plan also sets **temporary-resident thresholds** (a jurisdiction it shares with the federal government): for 2026, planned temporary foreign worker admissions of 40,400–55,700 and international student admissions of 44,500–68,500; by 2029 Québec targets a maximum stock of 65,000 temporary-worker permit holders and 110,000 international-student permit holders present in the province. This is new policy territory (Québec previously never capped temporary streams) and matters for the buyer-pipeline model since temporary residents are a large share of the demand funnel before PR/ownership.
- **Federal 2026-2028 Levels Plan (context, not Québec-specific):** national temporary-resident target cut sharply to 385,000 in 2026 (from 673,650 in 2025), 370,000/yr in 2027-28; national PR target held at 380,000/yr with economic-class share rising to 63-64%. Québec's own plan (45,000 PR/yr) is a subset of this national total and was tabled separately/later (Nov 2025) than the federal plan.
- **Granularity ceiling:** none of the IRCC/MIFI sources go below CMA (Montréal) for PR admissions, and MIFI's CSQ data is province-wide only (no CMA breakdown found for CSQ issuance specifically — settlement-destination-by-CMA only available via the federal IRCC PR-by-CMA table, which does not distinguish Québec-selected vs. federally-selected within Québec).

### 5. Mortality / decrement + seniors' housing-transition data

- **StatCan Life Tables, Canada, Provinces and Territories** (catalogue 84-537-X, e.g. 2025 edition covering data through ~2022-2023): complete single-year and 3-year-average life tables by province including Québec, with life expectancy, death probabilities (qx), survivors (lx), person-years lived. Free, downloadable, table 13-10-0114-01 (3-year estimates) and related mortality-rate-by-age tables (13-10-0710-01). Annual/biennial cadence historically, though single-year complete tables lag ~2 years.
- **ISQ mortality/life-expectancy tables:** parallel Québec-specific life-expectancy and mortality-by-age-and-cause data at province, administrative-region, and RMR level (statistique.quebec.ca "Deaths – Québec" and "Life expectancy" products), free.
- **Seniors' housing-transition rates (the actual "does an aging owner sell/downsize" input):** CMHC's Housing Market Insight / Observer series — most useful found item: CMHC's Nov 2023 "Senior Households' Impact on Canada's Real Estate Market" report, which reports that the historical property-selling rate for owners over age 75 fell from **38.6% (1996-2001 cohort)** to **36% (2016-2021 cohort)** — i.e., a directly usable age-75+ property-disposal/decrement rate, trending toward more aging-in-place. The same report notes Montréal seniors specifically skew toward moving into **rental** housing (vs. Toronto/Vancouver seniors skewing toward condo purchase) — a Montréal-specific behavioral parameter. CMHC also publishes a free, regularly updated "Senior-led Households" data table (Excel) at Canada/province/**CMA/CA level** (Montréal CMA included), covering tenure, mortgage status, dwelling type, income, by age-of-primary-maintainer, historical 2011-2021, most recently republished April 2025. Free download, no paywall.
- **LTC entry rates / aging-in-place granular flow data:** no dedicated machine-readable open dataset found giving province- or CMA-level long-term-care-entry hazard rates by age directly tied to housing-exit; existing public material is descriptive research/reports (CMHC Observer articles, ESDC "Report on Housing Needs of Seniors" 2019, FCM "Seniors and Housing" report) rather than a downloadable rate table. This is the weakest-instrumented leg of the model — expect to proxy LTC-entry-driven housing exits via the CMHC age-75+ selling-rate figure above combined with Census tenure-by-age cross-sections, rather than a purpose-built transition-rate dataset.

### 6. Housing stock / supply side

- **CMHC Housing Market Information Portal (HMIP):** starts, completions, and units-under-construction, free, monthly cadence, by region/province/**CMA**/dwelling type, for all urban centres ≥10,000 population (finer detail — under-construction inventory, absorption — for centres ≥50,000). Montréal CMA fully covered; no CT/borough-level breakdown from CMHC (CMA is the floor).
- **Ville de Montréal rôle d'évaluation foncière (open data, donnees.montreal.ca):** already known-available per the question — confirmed: "Unités d'évaluation foncière" dataset, free CSV/GeoJSON/Shapefile, parcel-level (matricule / MATRICULE83 property ID), fields include CUBF usage code (LIBELLE_UTILISATION), land area, assessment-unit category (regular vs. condo). This is StatCan/CMHC's finest possible geography (individual parcel, rollable up to any custom boundary including borough) but carries **zero owner-demographic fields** — no age, no immigration status, no name — by design (municipal assessment roll, not a persons registry). It is the supply-stock/parcel-inventory leg only; owner demographics must come from Census/CHSP-equivalent sources (which, per item 1, don't exist for Québec at the owner level).
- Provincial-level "Quebec property assessment rolls" (all-Québec, not just Montréal) also exist as bulk open data via open.canada.ca / données Québec, same CUBF/matricule/value/construction-year structure, refreshed annually.

### 7. Intergenerational transfer / inheritance data

- **StatCan Survey of Financial Security (SFS), 2023 wave** (most recent), analytical release in the Housing Statistics series (36-28-0001-2025003, "Familial support in entering the Canadian housing market"): reports **5% of families** living in a home acquired in full/part via gift or inheritance, **9%** reporting a gift/inheritance contributed to their down payment; generational comparisons show 1990s-born adults with homeowner parents were twice as likely to own by 2021 as those without, and under-35 owners who got parental help received an average **$85,000** gift. Age breakdowns available (<35, 35-44, 45-54, 55-64, 65+). **Geographic granularity: national only** — no province, no CMA, no Québec-specific breakout in this release (SFS microdata could in principle support a province-level custom tab via RDC access, but no such published breakdown exists).
- **Registre foncier du Québec — "Statistiques du Registre foncier du Québec sur le marché immobilier"** (open data, données Québec / open.canada.ca, monthly, free): counts of registered sales, **property transfers** (mutations — this is the literal ownership-transfer-volume series, including inheritance-driven and inter-vivos transfers, though the dataset does not separately flag transfer *reason*), mortgages, and financial-difficulty acts, by **administrative region** (not CMA/borough) and province-wide total. E.g., May 2026: 16,193 sales, 21,160 transfers, 28,335 mortgages registered province-wide. This is the closest thing to a genuine "transfer volume" proxy for Québec but does NOT decompose transfers by cause (sale vs. bequest vs. donation) or by buyer/seller age — it is a pure count series, administrative-region granularity, no demographic linkage.
- No StatCan or ISQ product found that publishes inheritance/bequest-driven property-transfer volumes specifically (as distinct from all-cause transfers) at sub-national geography. This is a genuine data gap: transfer-cause and transferor/transferee age would have to be inferred indirectly (e.g., cross-referencing Registre foncier transfer timing/frequency against the CMHC seniors' age-75+ selling-rate proxy and Census tenure-by-age changes over time), not pulled from a single authoritative source.

## Bottom line for model design

The Québec/Montréal build has **no CHSP-equivalent linked owner-demographic dataset** — the single biggest asset-side gap. The workable substitute stack is: (a) Census tenure-by-age-of-maintainer at CMA/CD level for free, custom-tabbed to CT level for a fee, to model the *selling cohort's* age profile; (b) ISQ household projections (RMR/MRC granularity, 2021-2071 horizon, 3 scenarios) to project *how many* aging households exist by area; (c) CMHC's age-75+ selling-rate (36-38.6%) and Montréal's rental-not-condo senior-transition skew as the behavioral decrement parameter; (d) IRCC PR-by-CMA + Québec MIFI CSQ/temporary-resident plans for the *buying* cohort's inflow volume (CMA granularity only, no CT); (e) Ville de Montréal's parcel-level rôle d'évaluation for the physical stock (plex counts, CUBF, construction year) at true parcel/borough granularity but with zero demographic content; (f) Registre foncier transfer-volume series as a coarse, cause-blind validation check on modeled turnover, at administrative-region granularity only. Every cross-tab that would directly answer "which aging-owner cohorts are transacting, where, and who is buying" requires either a paid StatCan custom tabulation (Census microdata) or acceptance that Québec simply sits outside StatCan's flagship owner-linkage product.
