# Translating the real world into the config

Exact keys, `required` flags and notes come from `uv run hde --print-schema`;
this table maps what a member of the public says onto them.

| The user says | Where it goes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| property tax, home/unit insurance, utilities the owner pays | `condo.other_recurring_costs` / `house.other_recurring_costs` `{name, annual_amount, escalation_rate}` | escalation is REAL (0 = tracks inflation); the engine warns when an owned option has none Before guessing, `uv run hde --print-anchors`: `property_tax.laval` / `.montreal` / `.quebec_city` / `.toronto` are rates on ASSESSED value (assessed ≠ market — say so), `school_tax.qc` is separate, `.gatineau` / `.ottawa` are explicit no-source entries, `home_insurance.qc` / `.on` are household-average FLOORS, not premiums; the read-back cites a match by name. On a price threshold use `property_tax_rate` (fraction of value) and `purchase_costs_rate` (fraction of price) instead of dollar figures, so they scale with the price |
| welcome / land-transfer tax | `land_transfer_tax: auto` + `province: QC \| ON` and, in those cities, `municipality: montreal \| toronto` (owned option, or `province` at the top level); `first_time_buyer: true` when they say they qualify | NEVER guess it as a % of price: the engine prices the published brackets, re-derives them at every `--sweep` / `--break-even` point, and adds the duty to `purchase_costs` — so it is netted from `cash_available` too. Montréal's schedule REPLACES Québec's; Toronto's is charged ON TOP of Ontario's. Rebates: Ontario $4,000, Toronto $4,475, each capped at its own leg's tax; neither Québec schedule has one anchored. Read the `purchase costs:` line back to the user |
| notary, inspection, mortgage-insurance premium paid in cash | `purchase_costs` (owned option) | paid at year 0, outside the affordability ratio; with `land_transfer_tax: auto` these are what is left to state |
| mortgage-insurance premium (<20% down) | `mortgage_insurance: auto` + `province: QC \| ON \| other` (owned option, or `province` at the top level) | NEVER compute the premium by hand: the engine picks the CMHC tier, adds the premium to the loan, and pays the provincial tax on it in cash — and re-derives the tier at every `--sweep` / `--break-even` point. Read the `insured:` clause of the `financing:` line back to the user. `financed_purchase_costs` remains the manual path for a premium you were quoted; the two are refused together |
| roof, appliances, special assessment, moving costs | `events` `{name, base_cost, expected_year}` | one-offs during the horizon; they DO enter that year's affordability ratio |
| realtor commission + notary at sale | `selling_cost_rate` (default 5%, WOWA 2026) | dominates short horizons |
| "we have $140k saved" (the cash for day one) | `cash_available` on the owned option — the engine nets `purchase_costs` into the down payment and prints the loan-to-value; never type a hand-computed `down_payment` from a cash pile |
| "it's in an index fund / ETFs" (where the renter's money sits) | `rent.investment_return_rate` (REAL) — an equity fund is not the engine's 3% default; label the rate you choose and bracket it |
| the money the renter keeps instead of buying | `rent.invested_down_payment` + `investment_return_rate` | the buyer's TOTAL year-0 cash (down payment + purchase costs), charged at year 0 and credited at its terminal value exactly like the buyer's; omitted = assume it earns the discount rate |
| a posted 5-year fixed rate | `mortgage_rate` = effective annual, `(1 + r/2)^2 − 1`, used AS IS in `mode: nominal`; `mortgage_term_years` is the AMORTIZATION (usually 25), never the 5-year term — when only the term product is quoted ("4.5% on a 5-year fixed") assume 25, label it in the intake as a default they can overrule (30 is allowed on an uninsured mortgage), never spend a follow-up on it | the rate is held for the whole amortization — renewal risk is not modelled (biases toward buying when rates are rising) |
| "prices here grow 3%", "rent goes up 3%", "my portfolio makes 6%" | `value_growth_rate`, `rent_escalation_rate`, `investment_return_rate` — a colloquial GROWTH or RETURN rate is a STICKER (nominal) figure unless the user says "above inflation": convert each to real, (1 + quoted)/(1 + 2.1%) − 1, before typing it (nominal mode composes inflation back on). The mortgage is the exception: its only conversion is compounding — never an inflation conversion | gate 3 |
| "I might move for work" | run the shorter horizon as a second config | no probabilistic exit in the engine |
| browsing a market, no specific property ("houses in <area> around $650k") | the price band is THEIR number (`--sweep` or `--break-even` across it); everything property-specific is an estimate you label — tax from the municipality's rate or "≈ x% of value (illustrative)", maintenance NAHB routine ≈ 0.6% of value or the examples' 1.2% (name which), fees typical for the building type, purchase costs from `land_transfer_tax: auto` plus notary — and the least certain one gets a `--sweep` | say which figures are estimates and that a real listing's tax bill and fees replace them |
| a demographic view for a Québec area | `market_scenario: {path: tests/fixtures/scenario_prior_golden.json, geography: …}` — `LAVAL_RA13` for Laval, `MTL_ISLAND_RA06` for the island, `MTL_RMR` for the rest of the metro, `QC_RMR` for Québec City, `HORS_RMR` outside any CMA | the prior ADDS its real drift to `value_growth_rate` in the Monte Carlo only; the deterministic line uses the base alone — with no user view leave the base at the engine default (0) so the prior is the growth view; a non-zero base is a second view stacked on the prior: label it and sweep it |
