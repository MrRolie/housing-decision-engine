# Translating the real world into the config

Exact keys, `required` flags and notes come from `uv run hde --print-schema`;
this table maps what a member of the public says onto them.

| The user says | Where it goes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| property tax, home/unit insurance, utilities the owner pays | `condo.other_recurring_costs` / `house.other_recurring_costs` `{name, annual_amount, escalation_rate}` | escalation is typed AS QUOTED like every rate (2.1% = tracks inflation; 0 = flat in sticker terms, a real decline; omitted = tracks inflation); the engine warns when an owned option has none. Before guessing, `uv run hde --print-anchors`: `property_tax.laval` / `.montreal` / `.quebec_city` / `.ottawa` / `.toronto` are rates on ASSESSED value (assessed ≠ market — say so; Ontario's 2026 base is January 2016), `school_tax.qc` is separate, `.gatineau` is an explicit no-source entry (the city taxes by neighbourhood unit), `home_insurance.qc` / `.on` are household-average FLOORS, not premiums; the read-back cites a match by name. On a price threshold use `property_tax_rate` (fraction of value) and `purchase_costs_rate` (fraction of price) instead of dollar figures, so they scale with the price an Ontario placeholder is checked DOWNWARD first (bills rest on a 2016 assessment base, so a rate on market price overstates them) |
| a Québec owner's tax bill | two `other_recurring_costs` lines, `municipal_tax` and `school_tax` — a Québec bill is BOTH (`property_tax.<city>` and `school_tax.qc` in `--print-anchors`, each on assessed value); one line that equals the pair is declared `anchor:property_tax.<city>+school_tax.qc` | a Québec line with the municipal rate alone understates the bill (favours buying) — the engine's coherence note says so; declare each recurring-cost line by its NAME in `sources:` (`uv run hde --print-schema` for the exact form) when the lines have different sources, the whole list as one entry only when they share one |
| welcome / land-transfer tax | `land_transfer_tax: auto` + `province: "QC" \| "ON"` — QUOTED: an unquoted `ON` is a YAML boolean and the engine refuses it — and, in those cities, `municipality: montreal \| toronto` (owned option, or `province` at the top level); `first_time_buyer: true` when they say they qualify | NEVER guess it as a % of price: the engine prices the published brackets, re-derives them at every `--sweep` / `--break-even` point, and adds the duty to `purchase_costs` — so it is netted from `cash_available` too. Montréal's schedule REPLACES Québec's; Toronto's is charged ON TOP of Ontario's. Rebates: Ontario $4,000, Toronto $4,475, each capped at its own leg's tax; Québec refunds nothing at closing — its first-home refundable tax credit (up to $5,875 of the duty, on the return; `land_transfer_tax.qc.first_time_buyer_credit_max`) is named by the line and never applied, and Montréal's own program closed to new applications on 2026-07-07. Read the `purchase costs:` line back to the user neither Québec schedule has one anchored. |
| notary, inspection, mortgage-insurance premium paid in cash | `purchase_costs` (owned option) | paid at year 0, outside the affordability ratio; with `land_transfer_tax: auto` these are what is left to state |
| mortgage-insurance premium (<20% down) | `mortgage_insurance: auto` + `province: "QC" \| "ON" \| "other"` (quoted; owned option, or `province` at the top level) | NEVER compute the premium by hand: the engine picks the CMHC tier, adds the premium to the loan, and pays the provincial tax on it in cash — and re-derives the tier at every `--sweep` / `--break-even` point. Read the `insured:` clause of the `financing:` line back to the user. `financed_purchase_costs` remains the manual path for a premium you were quoted; the two are refused together |
| roof, appliances, special assessment, moving costs | `events` `{name, base_cost, expected_year}` | one-offs during the horizon; they DO enter that year's affordability ratio |
| realtor commission + notary at sale | `selling_cost_rate` (default 5%, WOWA 2026) | dominates short horizons |
| "we have $140k saved" (the cash for day one) | `cash_available` on the owned option — the engine nets `purchase_costs` into the down payment and prints the loan-to-value; never type a hand-computed `down_payment` from a cash pile |
| "$X for the down payment plus $Y for closing" (split cash) | EITHER `down_payment: X` with `land_transfer_tax: auto` — the engine prices the duty; say where the rest of Y goes (notary and inspection into `purchase_costs`, any remainder unspent and said so) — OR `cash_available: X+Y` and let the engine net; state in the intake which you took | the two differ when Y misjudges the costs: with `down_payment: X` a shortfall in Y never touches the loan-to-value; with `cash_available` it lowers the down payment and can cross the 20% line — read the `financing:` line back either way |
| "I don't know if it's freehold or has a fee" (a townhouse) | two configs — a `condo` block with a labelled fee and a `house` block with a maintenance rate — both run and both quoted in the answer with their verdicts; never pick one silently | say which config the headline is from; the fee figure is a labelled estimate until they read it off the listing |
| "it's in an index fund / ETFs" (where the renter's money sits) | `rent.investment_return_rate`, the return as the fund quotes it — an equity fund is not the engine's 3% real (≈ 5.1% quoted) default; label the rate you choose and bracket it |
| the money the renter keeps instead of buying | `rent.invested_down_payment` + `investment_return_rate` | the buyer's TOTAL year-0 cash (down payment + purchase costs), charged at year 0 and credited at its terminal value exactly like the buyer's; omitted with a `tax.renter_capital` block it is DERIVED from that block's piles (read the derived figure back and check it against the buyer's year-0 cash); omitted with no block = assume it earns the discount rate |
| "it's in my TFSA / RRSP / FHSA / a regular account" (where the renter's money sits) | `tax.renter_capital: {tfsa, rrsp, fhsa, taxable}` in DOLLARS, summing to `rent.invested_down_payment`; `income.annual_income` plus a QUOTED top-level `province: "QC" \| "ON"` let the engine resolve the marginal rate from the published brackets — elsewhere type `tax.marginal_rate` as a fraction (0.3612), never converted | the taxable share earns the after-tax return (capital gains by default; `taxable_return_treatment: interest` for a savings account); the sheltered shares are untouched; the owner's principal-residence exemption is named. Without the block the engine WARNS that the return is untaxed (toward renting) — quote that warning under Not modelled; with it, read the `tax:` line back (the rate and its derivation, the split, the drag in dollars) |
| a first home with an FHSA, or an RRSP they would draw on (the Home Buyers' Plan) | `tax.fhsa: {balance, annual_contribution, years_until_purchase}` and `tax.hbp_withdrawal` (≤ $60,000 and ≤ the RRSP share) beside `first_time_buyer: true` on a FINANCED option and a `rent:` block; state `cash_available` WITHOUT the HBP amount — the engine adds it and the FHSA refunds to the down payment before the insurance tier | the refunds enter BOTH sides (the deduction does not depend on buying); the renter's FHSA share rolls to an RRSP and is haircut at the horizon (`tax.retirement_marginal_rate`, default = the current rate, labelled); the HBP's own cost is its repayment schedule — the `hbp:` line — zero when the return equals the discount rate; like-for-like is `cash_available + hbp_withdrawal = rent.invested_down_payment` (the engine warns otherwise). Read the `fhsa:` clause and the `hbp:` line back. `examples/first_time_buyer_montreal.yaml` is the worked end-to-end config for this case — the `tax:` block, the FHSA and the HBP together, with a `sources:` entry per key; the withdrawal joins EITHER `cash_available` or a stated `down_payment`, whichever the option carries |
| a posted 5-year fixed rate | `mortgage_rate` = the rate AS QUOTED — a Canadian 5-year rate compounds semi-annually and the engine converts it once to the effective annual rate it amortizes at (`mortgage_rate_compounding`, semi-annual by default), with both forms on the `rates:` line; never convert by hand. It is a contract rate: the inflation conversion never touches it in either mode. `mortgage_term_years` is the AMORTIZATION (usually 25), never the 5-year term — when only the term product is quoted ("4.5% on a 5-year fixed") assume 25, label it in the intake as a default they can overrule (30 is allowed on an uninsured mortgage), never spend a follow-up on it. The 5-year term itself is NOT discarded: it is `mortgage_renewal_years`, and it needs `mortgage_renewal_rates` beside it — one rate per renewal, or one figure for every renewal — which the engine anchors nothing for, so it is theirs to state or yours to label as an estimate | with no renewal path the rate is held for the whole amortization and renewal risk is not modelled (biases toward buying when rates are rising); with one, the payment steps at each renewal and the run prices it as ONE stated scenario |
| no mortgage quote yet ("whatever the bank gives us") | `mortgage_rate` = the contracted anchor as the base, picked by the TIER the run's `financing:` line prints, not by your own arithmetic on the cash — insured → `mortgage_rate.contracted_5y_insured`, uninsured → `mortgage_rate.contracted_5y_uninsured` (Bank of Canada, the observation date in `--print-anchors`); when the tier moves along a scan or sits within a premium of the 20% line, sweep both. `mortgage_rate.posted_5y` is the ceiling in a two-point `--sweep`; declare the base `anchor:<that name>` | a posted rate is a list price that contracted rates run under (the engine warns when a config types it as the quote); the verdict sentence names the rate it is at ("at the 4.35% contracted rate the Bank of Canada reports; at the 6.09% posted rate …") — never an unnamed rate |
| "prices here grow 3%", "rent goes up 3%", "my portfolio makes 6%" | `value_growth_rate`, `rent_escalation_rate`, `investment_return_rate` — type the QUOTED figure: the engine converts it once (deflated in real mode, used as typed in nominal mode) and the read-back's `rates:` line shows both forms — in nominal mode all three (`<key> 0.0% as quoted = 0.0% nominal = −2.1% real`); never convert by hand. "Above inflation" means the user gave a real figure — set `rates: real`. The mortgage's only conversion is its compounding, and the engine does that one too — never an inflation conversion | gate 3 |
| "prices stay flat", "I don't expect prices to move" | AMBIGUOUS under the sticker convention — state both readings in the intake and run the NEUTRAL one unless the user picks: flat in dollars is `value_growth_rate: 0` (0% quoted = a 2.1%/yr REAL decline); keeping up with inflation is 2.1% quoted, which is what OMITTING the key gives (the engine's neutral 0% real default) | a 0.0 you typed on a "flat" answer is `assistant` in `sources:`, never `user` — the user said a phrase, not a number. In nominal mode a typed 0% owned-option growth warns; quote it: `… is a 2.1%/yr REAL decline …; a "flat" view that tracks inflation is 2.1% quoted — or omit the key for the neutral 0% real default` |
| "I might move for work" | run the shorter horizon as a second config | no probabilistic exit in the engine |
| browsing a market, no specific property ("houses in <area> around $650k") | the price band is THEIR number (`--sweep` or `--break-even` across it); everything property-specific is an estimate you label — tax from the municipality's rate or "≈ x% of value (illustrative)", maintenance NAHB routine ≈ 0.6% of value or the examples' 1.2% (name which), fees typical for the building type, purchase costs from `land_transfer_tax: auto` plus notary — and the least certain one gets a `--sweep` | say which figures are estimates and that a real listing's tax bill and fees replace them |
| a demographic view for a Québec area | `market_scenario: {path: tests/fixtures/scenario_prior_golden.json, geography: …}` — `LAVAL_RA13` for Laval, `MTL_ISLAND_RA06` for the island, `MTL_RMR` for the rest of the metro, `QC_RMR` for Québec City, `HORS_RMR` outside any CMA | the prior ADDS its real drift to `value_growth_rate` in the Monte Carlo only; the deterministic line uses the base alone — with no user view leave the base at the engine default (0) so the prior is the growth view; a non-zero base is a second view stacked on the prior: label it and sweep it |
